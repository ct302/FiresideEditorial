## The Weight of Wood

There is something profoundly grounding about selecting a Yule log—feeling its heft in your hands, tracing the grain that tells of decades of slow growth, rings like whispered secrets. The oldest traditions speak of the log being chosen in silence, never spoken over, carried home without wheels (lest the luck scatter). You bring it in with reverence, placing it carefully on the hearth as if setting an honored guest at the table.

The scent of seasoned oak or hickory beginning to warm is its own kind of prayer—smoky, resinous, ancient. In that first moment when flame kisses wood, time seems to fold inward. The crackle isn't just sound; it's conversation. Between past and present. Between the living and the remembered.

## Light Against the Long Dark

Our ancestors knew the Yule log not as decoration but as survival. The winter solstice—the year's longest night—demanded every advantage against the cold. The log must burn all night, and whoever kept it burning earned the year's goodwill from the household spirits.

Modern hearths may be electric or gas, but the ritual persists. A candle shaped like a log on your mantel, a small bundle of pine branches tied with twine, even the simple act of lighting a fire on the darkest evening—these carry the old power.

The Yule log promises return. As it burns down through the night, it whispers that light endures. That warmth can be summoned from seemingly nothing. That even the longest dark yields to morning.

## The Quiet After

When the log has burned to embers, something shifts in the room. The air tastes different—softer, settled. Children born since last Yule have grown a little taller in the watching. Conversations that began in October have deepened into something more honest by firelight.

Some families save the ashes, scattering them in spring as blessing on the garden. Others keep a fragment of the original log, replanting it when new growth appears, ensuring next year's flame carries this year's memory.

> "A fire is never just a fire. It is the captured sun, held in the keeping of those who remember how to tend it."

We are all ember-keepers now. The logs we choose, the flames we nurture, the darkness we push back—one careful burn at a time.

The hearth remembers everything. And in its glow, so do we.
