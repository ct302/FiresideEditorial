## The First Spark

You strike the match, and the sulfur scent rises — sharp, brief, then swallowed by the sudden bloom of flame. The kindling catches, dry pine hissing, and the room leans into that first warm breath. There is a particular hush that settles over a house when the hearth wakes: the refrigerator stops its hum, the floorboards cease their creaking, even the cat pauses mid-stretch, ears twitching at the new crackle.

> The fire does not ask for attention; it simply *is*, and in its presence, you remember how to be still.

## The Log That Remembers

The Yule log is not merely wood. It is a chosen piece — often oak, sometimes ash or birch — saved from the previous year’s fire, its charred ends wrapped in cloth and tucked beneath the stairs or in the pantry. You carry it in both hands, feeling the weight of last winter’s stories pressed into its grain. When you lay it across the andirons, the wood sighs, releasing resins that smell of rain on hot stone and the sweet, smoky perfume of holidays past.

In some households, the log is anointed with wine or cider, a splash of honey, a sprinkle of salt — each gesture a whisper of gratitude for light returning. The flames climb, licking the bark, and the log begins its slow, deliberate burn. It will last through the longest night, through the telling of tales and the clink of mugs, through the silence between carols.

## Ashes and Promises

Morning finds the hearth a bed of white ash, the log reduced to a skeleton of glowing coals. You scoop the ash into a tin, not for discard but for the garden — potash for the roses, a dusting over the vegetable beds. The cycle closes: fire feeds earth, earth feeds tree, tree becomes next year’s log.

There is a quiet promise in that ash. It says the dark is not endless. It says we have kept the flame, and the flame has kept us. As you brush the hearth clean, the air still carries the ghost of smoke — pine, oak, a hint of cinnamon from last night’s mulled cider — and you understand: the ritual was never about the wood. It was about the waiting, the watching, the willingness to tend something that burns without asking why.
