You’ll find me sitting on a chipped bench in the heart of the forest, a thermos of cocoa steaming beside me. The air smells of pine resin and dry leaves, and the only sound is the crunch of frost beneath my boots. Boxing Day walks used to be my uncle Tom’s thing—he’d say they were ‘for the blood’ or something. Now I’m not sure if that’s metaphorical or literal, but I’ve learned to clear my mind by the time I reach the old oak. 

### The Quiet Call of the Forest

There’s a magic in planning a Boxing Day walk. Unlike the frantic holiday prep, this is slow. You check the weather app—maybe a hailstorm is creeping in—then pack a wool hat, a pair of waterproof socks, and a thermos with something sweeter than coffee. My favorite moment is stringing the headlights together, those tiny fairy-lights from last Christmas. They flicker as you hike, casting a warm glow on frost-tinged branches. It’s not about speed or distance. It’s about noticing: the way light filters through the trees, the silence that lets your breath sting your cheeks, the tug on your wool scarf when you pass a woodpecker carving out its nest.

> *‘A winter walk is a gift you give yourself, not someone else,’* my aunt once wrote in a postcard from the Lake District. It’s still pinned to my fridge.

### Packing with Purpose

You don’t need much, but what you do bring matters. My father taught me to bring a small notebook just in case. For sketching a fox’s footprints or scribbling names of birds. Sometimes I forget, and it’s okay—sometimes the best memories are just the feel of snow tickling your socks. That said, a compass is a must. The woods around here shift in winter, and you wouldn’t want to find yourself lost with only a smartphone (though I’ll admit, I do carry one for emergencies).

The best walks end with cocoa by a fire or a hearth. If you’re lucky, you’ll encounter other walkers. A shared thermos, swapped tales of stag sightings—it’s these small exchanges that turn solitude into companionship.

Perhaps what I stumbled into was a tradition. Boxing Day has always been a day to linger, to pause. Maybe that’s why the forest calls. Not just for movement, but for stillness. When you exit the woods, you don’t just shed layers. You carry a little piece of the stillness with you, even as the day’s chaos begins again.

**This Christmas, let the forest remind you what tranquility feels like.**
